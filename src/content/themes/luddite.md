Today the word is mostly an insult, branding a critic of a technology as afraid of progress. In the AI debate it is aimed at those who warn of lost jobs or want to slow AI down. Some critics now take the name with pride.

The first Luddites were textile workers, among them weavers and framework knitters, in Nottinghamshire, Yorkshire and Lancashire. From 1811 they smashed knitting frames and power looms in the name of a mythical leader, General Ned Ludd or King Ludd. In 1812 machine-breaking became a capital crime, and the unrest went on for about five more years.

The writer Brian Merchant and others argue that they fought wage cuts and the use of machines against workers, not machines as such. The word moved into the AI debate with the jobs question, and with warnings that AI might be dangerous.
