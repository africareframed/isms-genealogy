lead: Self-determination asks who decides how AI uses people's data and shapes their future, and the isms split between voice, markets, personal choice and exit.
isms:
  - ism: transhumanism
    text: Self-determination starts with one's own body and mind, which each person should be free to change. The Transhumanist Declaration, in its 2009 version, favours “wide personal choice”, from memory aids and life extension to cryonics.
  - ism: singularitarianism
    text: Sam Altman wants superintelligence “cheap, widely available, and not too concentrated”, with user freedom inside bounds that society decides. Richard Sutton doubts anyone can steer it, since humanity has no single dominant view.
  - ism: rationalism
    text: It asks that the public, not the labs alone, decide whether superintelligence is built. The Future of Life Institute's 2025 statement seeks a ban until there is broad scientific agreement and strong public support.
  - ism: effective-altruism
    text: Its longtermists fear that AI could lock in one set of values for a very long time, deciding for every generation to come. William MacAskill makes the warning in What We Owe the Future (2022).
  - ism: effective-accelerationism
    text: "Self-determination means exit: leaving a state instead of reforming it. Balaji Srinivasan's network state would crowdfund territory and seek recognition as a new country."
  - ism: techno-optimism
    text: Markets, not planners, should decide, since knowledge is spread among many people, as Hayek argued. Reid Hoffman's bloomers want AI in many hands, shaped by its users' feedback.
strands:
  - name: Indigenous data sovereignty
    text: "In 2019 the International Indigenous Data Sovereignty Interest Group set out the CARE Principles: collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics. They add a focus on people and purpose to the data-centred FAIR principles of open science. Their aim is that users of Indigenous data “Be FAIR and CARE”."
    source: International Indigenous Data Sovereignty Interest Group, Research Data Alliance, CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, September 2019, via the Global Indigenous Data Alliance, gida-global.org; Stephanie Russo Carroll et al., “The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance”, Data Science Journal 19(1), article 43, 4 November 2020
  - name: Māori data sovereignty
    text: "Te Hiku Media, a Māori non-profit broadcaster in New Zealand, built speech-to-text tools for te reo Māori from recordings by native speakers. Its Kaitiakitanga licence for outside partners keeps data sovereignty with local communities and bars uses that surveil or discriminate. In 2022 ITU News quoted its chief executive, Peter-Lucas Jones: “Data is the new land.”"
    source: ITU News, “How AI is helping revitalise indigenous languages”, itu.int, 9 August 2022
  - name: Plurality
    text: Audrey Tang, Taiwan's first digital minister, and the economist Glen Weyl argue that digital tools can strengthen democracy across social differences. Their 2024 book charts a path “between the extremes of techno-libertarianism and centralized AI governance”. Written with a community of contributors, it is dedicated to the public domain.
    source: "E. Glen Weyl, Audrey Tang and the Plurality Community, Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy, 2024, plurality.net (CC0); Wilson Center, “Plurality: Technology and the Future of Democracy”, 11 October 2024"
  - name: Citizens' assemblies
    text: Sixty randomly selected Belgians deliberated on AI for their country's EU presidency in 2024, asking for “every human in the loop”. Forty residents of French-speaking Switzerland, chosen by lot, followed in 2025. In 2023 Anthropic and the Collective Intelligence Project trained a model on a constitution drafted with about 1,000 Americans.
    source: "Connected by Data, “Belgian Citizens' Panel on AI – Launch Event”, 25 May 2024; Belgian Federal Public Service Foreign Affairs, “Citizen's panel on AI issues”, May 2024; EPFL AI Center, “Citizens' Assembly on AI”, and Mélissa Anchisi, “AI, good servant, bad master”, EPFL, 10 February 2026; Anthropic, “Collective Constitutional AI: Aligning a Language Model with Public Input”, 17 October 2023"
  - name: Exit and the network state
    text: "Balaji Srinivasan's The Network State (2022) proposes online communities that crowdfund territory and seek recognition as new countries. It builds on the idea of exit, leaving a system instead of reforming it. In 2024 Divya Siddarth, Anne-Marie Slaughter and Glen Weyl replied: “Exit is a necessary right, but it requires something to exit to.”"
    source: Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, 4 July 2022, thenetworkstate.com; Divya Siddarth, Anne-Marie Slaughter and E. Glen Weyl, “Build Network Societies, Not Network States”, Combinations, 13 November 2024, reposted by the Collective Intelligence Project as “We need network societies, not network states”, cip.org

The demand comes from many directions. Indigenous peoples claim authority over data about their lands, languages and communities. Democratic reformers want citizens, not only companies, to set the rules for AI.

The isms give their own answers. Transhumanists stress personal choice over body and mind, accelerationists the right to exit, and rationalists a public say over whether superintelligence is built.
