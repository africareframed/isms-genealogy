lead: The promise that AI will make intelligence, energy and goods so cheap that scarcity ends. Almost every ism in Silicon Valley makes it. They differ on when it comes, who gets it, and what it costs.
isms:
  - ism: transhumanism
    text: An abundance of life itself, with the end of ageing and a body and mind with no fixed limits. The Extropians of the 1990s added the faith in boundless growth.
  - ism: singularitarianism
    text: Abundance arrives with the singularity. As machine intelligence compounds, the cost of almost everything falls towards zero.
  - ism: rationalism
    text: Abundance is the prize if alignment is solved. A misaligned superintelligence would share none of it, so the prize can wait.
  - ism: effective-altruism
    text: The greatest abundance is a vast future, with trillions of lives across space and time. That is why the risk of extinction matters more than anything else.
  - ism: effective-accelerationism
    text: Abundance is what the universe tends towards when energy and intelligence are allowed to grow. Slowing that growth is the real harm.
  - ism: techno-optimism
    text: Technology plus markets makes abundance. Marc Andreessen's 2023 manifesto borrows Nick Land's phrase for it, the “techno-capital machine”.
strands:
  - name: Post-work
    text: The hope that automation frees people from jobs, and the fear that it simply removes them. The debate runs from John Maynard Keynes's essay on “our grandchildren” to today's arguments about AI and employment.
    source: J. M. Keynes, “Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren”, 1930
  - name: Universal basic income
    text: A guaranteed income as the way to share the gains of automation. Sam Altman funded one of the largest studies of it in the United States.
    source: OpenResearch unconditional cash study, results 2024
  - name: Fully automated luxury communism
    text: The left-wing version of the same promise. Abundance is possible, but only if the machines are publicly owned.
    source: Aaron Bastani, Fully Automated Luxury Communism, Verso, 2019
  - name: The abundance agenda
    text: Same word, different meaning. In US politics since 2025, “abundance” means building more housing, energy and infrastructure. It is about permits and planning, not AI.
    source: Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson, Abundance, 2025

The hope is old, and so is the question that comes with it: if machines do the work, who owns what they make? Peter Diamandis and Steven Kotler made abundance a Silicon Valley slogan with their 2012 book of that name. Sam Altman's essays have since tied it directly to AI, from “Moore's Law for Everything” (2021) to “The Gentle Singularity” (2025).
