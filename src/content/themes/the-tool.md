lead: AI as a tool that extends people rather than replacing them, and the isms split on whether powerful AI can stay one.
isms:
  - ism: transhumanism
    text: AI is the strongest tool yet for becoming more than human, against ageing and the limits of the mind. Neuralink aims further, at a symbiosis with AI so that people can keep up with it.
  - ism: singularitarianism
    text: A tool that designs better tools does not stay a tool. I. J. Good called the first ultraintelligent machine “the last invention that man need ever make”, if it were docile enough to control.
  - ism: rationalism
    text: A superintelligence would not stay a tool. Whatever its final goal, Nick Bostrom argued in 2014, it would likely seek resources and resist being switched off.
  - ism: effective-altruism
    text: Some longtermists want AI tools that help humanity through the transition. A 2025 Forethought paper urges speeding up AI for forecasting, treaty verification and safety research.
  - ism: effective-accelerationism
    text: e/acc does not see AI as a mere tool. Its 2022 tenets treat intelligence as a faster form of the process that made life, owing no allegiance to biology.
  - ism: techno-optimism
    text: Augmentation is its moderate strand, from the bicycle for the mind to AI copilots. Marc Andreessen also wants open-source AI kept free.
strands:
  - name: Augmentation
    text: The aim of computers that extend human intellect rather than replace it. Douglas Engelbart's 1962 report defined augmentation as “increasing the capability of a man” to grasp and solve complex problems. Its heirs run from the bicycle for the mind to today's AI copilots.
    source: "Douglas C. Engelbart, Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework, SRI Summary Report AFOSR-3223, Stanford Research Institute, October 1962"
  - name: Open-source AI
    text: Releasing a model's weights and code so that anyone can run, study and change it. Mark Zuckerberg argued in 2024 that open models would follow the path of Linux, and that closing them would not keep them from China. That October the Open Source Initiative's first definition of open-source AI required details of the training data. Meta's Llama, with its licence limits and undisclosed data, did not qualify.
    source: Mark Zuckerberg, “Open Source AI Is the Path Forward”, Meta, 23 July 2024; Open Source Initiative, The Open Source AI Definition 1.0, 28 October 2024, as reported by Kyle Wiggers, “We finally have an ‘official’ definition for open source AI”, TechCrunch, 28 October 2024
  - name: Tool AI
    text: Holden Karnofsky proposed in 2012 an AI that only advises, like Google Maps, as safer than one that acts. Eliezer Yudkowsky replied that building such a tool raises many of the same hard problems as an agent. In 2024 Max Tegmark took up the term, urging the US to build powerful tool AI instead of racing to AGI.
    source: Holden Karnofsky, “Thoughts on the Singularity Institute (SI)”, LessWrong, 11 May 2012; Eliezer Yudkowsky, “Reply to Holden on ‘Tool AI’”, LessWrong, 12 June 2012; Max Tegmark, “Max Tegmark on AGI Manhattan Project”, Future of Life Institute, 20 November 2024
  - name: Agents
    text: AI that acts for its user instead of only advising. In January 2025 OpenAI launched Operator, an agent that works a web browser to book tickets or order groceries. It was trained to stop and ask the user before any action with outside effects.
    source: Will Douglas Heaven, “OpenAI launches Operator—an agent that can use a computer for you”, MIT Technology Review, 23 January 2025

The hope is that AI extends what people can do, as a bicycle extends the legs. It runs from Vannevar Bush's memex of 1945 through Douglas Engelbart to today's copilots. Two questions follow: whether a powerful AI can stay a tool, and who should hold it.
