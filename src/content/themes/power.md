strands:
  - name: AI nationalism
    text: The view that AI will reshape the balance of power between nations, which the investor Ian Hogarth named in 2018. In 2024 Leopold Aschenbrenner, formerly of OpenAI, predicted a US government project to build AGI ahead of China. That November a US government commission proposed a programme modelled on the Manhattan Project to develop AGI.
    source: Ian Hogarth, “AI Nationalism”, ianhogarth.com, 2018; Leopold Aschenbrenner, “Situational Awareness”, 2024; Reuters, “US government commission pushes Manhattan Project-style AI initiative”, 20 November 2024
  - name: State-led AI
    text: In July 2017 China's State Council issued its New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan. It aims to make China “the world's primary AI innovation center” by 2030, with a core AI industry worth over 1 trillion yuan. The state plans and guides, the market leads, and AI is to move both ways between military and civilian uses.
    source: State Council of China, New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan, 2017, translated by Rogier Creemers, Graham Webster, Paul Triolo and Elsa Kania, DigiChina, Stanford University
  - name: The populist right
    text: On the American right, boosters such as David Sacks and Marc Andreessen want to build fast and win the AI race with China. Populists such as Steve Bannon and Josh Hawley worry about lost jobs, Big Tech power and harm to children. In July 2025 the Senate voted 99 to 1 to drop a ten-year ban on state AI laws, a move Bannon had pressed for.
    source: Billy Perrigo and Andrew R. Chow, “Senators Reject 10-Year Ban on State-Level AI Regulation, In Blow to Big Tech”, TIME, 1 July 2025; Luke Nicastro, “How AI Is Splitting the Right”, The American Conservative, 22 August 2026
  - name: Global rules
    text: Attempts to govern AI across borders. In November 2023, 28 countries and the EU, the US and China among them, signed the Bletchley Declaration on the risks of frontier AI. At the Paris summit in February 2025, 60 countries, China among them, signed a new declaration, but the US and the UK did not.
    source: "AFP, “UK, US, China sign safety pledge at AI summit”, 2 November 2023; Al Jazeera, “Paris AI summit: Why won't US, UK sign global artificial intelligence pact?”, 12 February 2025"

The question is who decides what AI becomes: the companies that build it, the states that host them, or a treaty above both. Since China's 2017 plan, more governments treat AI as a matter of national strength. In 2025 the White House titled its own plan “Winning the AI Race”.

The isms divide on it too. Rationalists ask states to halt the race together, techno-optimists warn against losing it to China, and neoreactionaries would leave the state behind.
