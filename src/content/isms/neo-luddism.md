Its writers want workers and citizens, not tech companies, to decide which machines to accept, AI included. The Luddites, they argue, fought not machines but their use against workers. Today they oppose AI that cuts jobs, takes artists' work and harvests attention, and many gather offline to build alternatives to Big Tech.

The name comes from English textile workers who broke machines from 1811 in the name of Ned Ludd. In 1990 the psychologist Chellis Glendinning published a neo-Luddite manifesto: all technologies are political, she wrote, and neo-Luddites are not anti-technology. In his account of the neo-Luddites, Kirkpatrick Sale put Lewis Mumford first among the critics behind them, with Jacques Ellul and Ivan Illich.

The rise of AI revived the name. In 2023 Brian Merchant's Blood in the Machine tied the Luddites to the Hollywood writers' strike and the fight over automation. Student Luddite clubs, the podcast This Machine Kills and New York's Summer of Ludd now wear the name with pride.
