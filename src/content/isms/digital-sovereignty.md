Its advocates want countries, cities and publics to take back AI's chips, clouds, data and models from a few mostly American firms. Their tools are public investment, procurement rules such as Buy European, open-source software and breaking up monopolies. Most of its voices are European, and some warn that the continent has become a digital colony.

In 2013 a French senator asked whether Europe was becoming a colony of the digital world. Months later Edward Snowden's leaks made US surveillance a European worry. Barcelona made technological sovereignty city policy in 2016, and France and Germany launched the Gaia-X cloud project in 2020.

From American antitrust, Lina Khan and Tim Wu revived Louis Brandeis's case against “the curse of bigness”. As chair of the US Federal Trade Commission, Khan turned it on Big Tech's investments in AI. Advocates of public AI want models built by public institutions, such as Switzerland's Apertus (2025).
