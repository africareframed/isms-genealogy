The hope is old: myths of immortality, Renaissance praise of human self-making, Enlightenment faith in endless improvement. The biologist Julian Huxley gave the word its modern sense in 1957. The movement took shape in the 1980s and 1990s, first around the Extropians in California, then, from 1998, the World Transhumanist Association, today called Humanity+.

AI sits at its centre twice over. It is the tool that could cure ageing and enhance the mind, and it may be a path for the mind itself, through brain–computer interfaces or uploading. Many transhumanists also take the risks of AI seriously, and the movement shades into singularitarianism, rationalism and effective altruism.
