Acceleration · Introduction

Effective accelerationism

Includes neoreaction and the network state

Effective accelerationism holds that the universe favours the growth of energy and intelligence, so AI should be accelerated, not slowed.

It began in 2022 among pseudonymous posters on Twitter, now X, including “Beff Jezos”, later named as the physicist Guillaume Verdon. Its name plays on effective altruism, and its physics borrows Jeremy England’s idea that matter organises itself to dissipate energy. Its older source is Nick Land, a Warwick philosopher in the 1990s, who saw capitalism as an intelligence to speed up.

Land also links e/acc to neoreaction. His 2012 essay The Dark Enlightenment took up Curtis Yarvin’s case against democracy and for exit, leaving a state instead of reforming it. Balaji Srinivasan’s network state turns exit into a plan, for online communities that crowdfund territory and seek recognition as new countries.

Who holds it now

Guillaume Verdon

Physicist and former quantum-computing researcher at Google. As “Beff Jezos” he co-wrote e/acc's first posts in 2022. His company Extropic builds thermodynamic computing chips.

Garry Tan

President and chief executive of the start-up accelerator Y Combinator. One of the investors who put e/acc on their X profiles in 2023.

Balaji Srinivasan

Investor and former chief technology officer of Coinbase. Wrote The Network State (2022). His Network School lost its Malaysian licence in 2026 and plans a new campus in Kazakhstan.

Curtis Yarvin

Software developer who blogged as Mencius Moldbug. Argues that American democracy should give way to a monarchy run like a company. The Washington Post named Marc Andreessen and Peter Thiel among his better-known readers in 2025.

Nick Land

Philosopher, formerly at the University of Warwick. e/acc cites his accelerationism, and his 2012 essay gave neoreaction its other name, the Dark Enlightenment. A 2026 New Yorker profile called him Silicon Valley's favourite doomsaying philosopher.

Who disputes it

Zvi Mowshowitz, who blogs on AI risk, believes e/acc's founder sees human extinction as an acceptable outcome. Most who use the label do not, he adds (2023). Émile P. Torres places e/acc in the TESCREAL bundle and faults its move from what the universe does to what people should do (2023). George Michael, a professor of criminal justice, finds Yarvin's worldview not explicitly fascist but at times of “a fascistic bent”. He adds that Yarvin has echoed racist views, though Yarvin says he is not a white nationalist (2022). Against the network state, Divya Siddarth, Glen Weyl and Anne-Marie Slaughter propose plural, participatory “network societies”, arguing that exit needs something to exit to.

2020s

2026Milestone · the network state

The Network School moves to Kazakhstan

Balaji Srinivasan

Srinivasan opened the Network School in 2024 at Forest City in Johor, Malaysia, across the strait from Singapore. It is a co-living community for founders and creators, part of his effort to build a network state. In July 2026 the local council revoked its business licence, citing breaches of licensing and premises rules. The next day Srinivasan signed a five-year memorandum with Kazakhstan's Ministry of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Development, and announced a new campus there.

Angelica Ang, “Booted from Malaysia, tech guru Balaji Srinivasan finds a new patron for his ‘nomad’ commune: Kazakhstan”, Fortune, 26 July 2026; Nan Hidayat Nan Azmie, “Network School heading to Kazakhstan after Johor operations halted”, The Star, 22 July 2026

2025Milestone

Thermodynamic chips

Extropic, led by Guillaume Verdon

Verdon's start-up, founded in 2022 with Trevor McCourt, unveils its first chips and a development platform already tested by early partners. The chips are built from probabilistic bits, circuits whose output wanders at random between 1 and 0 with programmable odds. In simulations on small benchmarks, Extropic claims, its approach could be up to 10,000 times more energy-efficient than GPUs. Once it succeeds, it says, “energy constraints will no longer limit AI scaling”.

Extropic, “Thermodynamic computing from zero to one” and “Inside X0 and XTR-0”, extropic.ai, 2025; Extropic, “Thermodynamic Hardware”, extropic.ai; Maximilian Schreiner, “Startup Extropic plans to revolutionize AI hardware by embracing the power of randomness”, The Decoder, 2 April 2024

2023Milestone

Beff Jezos is named

Guillaume Verdon

Forbes reports that the account @BasedBeffJezos is run by Guillaume Verdon, a physicist who had worked on quantum computing at Google. He had founded Extropic, a start-up building hardware for AI. Verdon then spoke under his own name, including on Lex Fridman's podcast. Marc Andreessen and Garry Tan had already put e/acc on their X profiles, and Andreessen's manifesto named @BasedBeffJezos a patron saint.

Emily Baker-White, “Who Is @BasedBeffJezos, The Leader Of The Tech Elite's ‘E/Acc’ Movement?”, Forbes, 1 December 2023, as reported in Josh Scott, BetaKit, 8 December 2023, and Kevin Roose, “This A.I. Subculture's Motto: Go, Go, Go”, The New York Times, 10 December 2023; Steve Mollman, “Jeff Bezos and Elon Musk are shaping our future in outer space. ‘Beff Jezos’ says the billionaires have earned the right”, Fortune, 30 December 2023; Marc Andreessen, “The Techno-Optimist Manifesto”, a16z, 16 October 2023

2022Modern definition

“Notes on e/acc principles and tenets”

Beff Jezos and Bayeslord

In point-form notes on Substack, the two set out the thinking behind e/acc. Life arose through dissipative adaptation, they argue, and intelligence is a faster form of the same process, capitalism a form of intelligence. Against top-down control they favour competition, and welcome Balaji Srinivasan's network states as tests of how to govern. Some in e/acc, Beff among them, call themselves post-humanists, and their summary says to stop fighting “the thermodynamic will of the universe”.

Beff Jezos and Bayeslord, “Notes on e/acc principles and tenets”, Beff's Newsletter, Substack, 10 July 2022

2022Milestone · the network state

The Network State

Balaji Srinivasan

The former chief technology officer of Coinbase publishes his book on 4 July, also free to read online. A network state, he writes, is “a highly aligned online community with a capacity for collective action”. It crowdfunds territory around the world and in time wins diplomatic recognition from existing states. Six days later, e/acc's tenets said the idea particularly excited them.

Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, 2022, thenetworkstate.com; Balaji Srinivasan, “The Network State Book”, balajis.com, 19 March 2022, updated 4 July 2022; Beff Jezos and Bayeslord, “Notes on e/acc principles and tenets”, 10 July 2022

2022Coined the word

Effective accelerationism is named

“swarthy” (@zestular), with @BasedBeffJezos, @bayeslord and @creatine_cycle

At the end of May, a handful of pseudonymous Twitter users announce a new philosophy, its name a play on effective altruism. A newsletter post by swarthy defines it and sets it apart from EA, which it finds unfocused and too averse to risk. Its first line repeats a sentence from Nick Land's 2017 introduction to accelerationism. Humanity's overarching goal, it says, is “to preserve the light of consciousness”.

swarthy, newsletter post on Substack (swarthy.substack.com), 31 May 2022, reposted verbatim as “Effective Accelerationism — e/acc”, e/acc newsletter, 31 October 2022; “e/acc (Effective Accelerationism)”, Know Your Meme; Hasan Chowdhury, “Get the lowdown on ‘e/acc’ …”, Business Insider, 28 July 2023; Nick Land, “A Quick-and-Dirty Introduction to Accelerationism”, Jacobite, 25 May 2017

2010s

2013Milestone · the network state

“Silicon Valley's Ultimate Exit”

Balaji Srinivasan

At Y Combinator's Startup School, a founder of the genetic-testing company Counsyl contrasts two responses to a declining system. Voice means changing it from within, and exit means leaving to build something new. “Exit amplifies voice,” he argues, and technology should help people opt out peacefully. Reason noted that he was echoing Albert O. Hirschman's 1970 book Exit, Voice, and Loyalty.

Balaji Srinivasan, “Silicon Valley's Ultimate Exit”, Startup School, Y Combinator, October 2013; J. D. Tuccille, “Tech Should Make It Easier To Escape Government Control, Says Startup Veteran Balaji Srinivasan”, Reason, 30 October 2013; Y Combinator, “Videos from Startup School 2013 are now online”, 27 October 2013

2013Claimed ancestor · cited

Dissipation-driven adaptation

Jeremy England

An MIT physicist derives the least heat that any self-replicating system must give off. He goes further, arguing that matter driven by an outside energy source will often reorganise itself to dissipate more energy. Harvard's Eugene Shakhnovich found it interesting but “extremely speculative” as an account of life. e/acc's 2022 tenets cite England for their claim that life emerged through dissipative adaptation.

Jeremy L. England, “Statistical Physics of Self-Replication”, The Journal of Chemical Physics 139 (12), 121923, 2013; Natalie Wolchover, “A New Physics Theory of Life”, Quanta Magazine, 22 January 2014; Beff Jezos and Bayeslord, “Notes on e/acc principles and tenets”, 2022

2012Milestone · neoreaction

The Dark Enlightenment

Nick Land

In a long essay posted online in parts, Land surveys the blogger Mencius Moldbug and his readers, whom he calls neo-reactionaries. Its first part is headed “Neo-reactionaries head for the exit”. Against democratic voice it sets exit, the freedom to leave, and it adopts Moldbug's name for the ruling order, the Cathedral. It also quotes Peter Thiel's doubt that freedom and democracy are compatible.

Nick Land, “The Dark Enlightenment”, thedarkenlightenment.com, 2012, archived copy

2000s

2009Claimed ancestor · neoreaction

“The Education of a Libertarian”

Peter Thiel

In an essay for Cato Unbound, the PayPal co-founder writes, “I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible.” The great task for libertarians, he argues, is to find a way out of politics altogether. He looks to cyberspace, outer space and the sea, and backs Patri Friedman's seasteading experiment. Nick Land quotes the line in The Dark Enlightenment and calls the exchange a milestone.

Peter Thiel, “The Education of a Libertarian”, Cato Unbound, 13 April 2009; quoted in Nick Land, “The Dark Enlightenment”, 2012

2007Milestone · neoreaction

Unqualified Reservations

Curtis Yarvin, as Mencius Moldbug

A software developer opens a blog under a pen name with “A Formalist Manifesto”. Over the next years he argues that democracy should give way to rule by a monarch, perhaps modelled on a corporate chief executive. He calls the universities and press that, he says, really hold power “the Cathedral”. In 2013 Peter Thiel invests in his software company, Tlon.

Mencius Moldbug [Curtis Yarvin], “A Formalist Manifesto”, Unqualified Reservations, 2007; George Michael, “An antidemocratic philosophy called ‘neoreaction’ is creeping into GOP politics”, The Conversation, July 2022; Harrison Smith and Roger Burrows, “Software, Sovereignty and the Post-Neoliberal Politics of Exit”, Theory, Culture & Society 38 (6), 2021

1990s

1997Attributed by historians · exit

The Sovereign Individual

James Dale Davidson and William Rees-Mogg

Its authors predict that the internet and digital money will let the richest and most able slip free of the nation state. Citizenship will become obsolete, they argue, and the welfare state will collapse. Peter Thiel wrote the preface to a 2020 edition. The scholars Harrison Smith and Roger Burrows argue that it prefigured neoreaction's politics of exit.

James Dale Davidson and William Rees-Mogg, The Sovereign Individual, Simon & Schuster, 1997; review, Publishers Weekly, February 1997; Quinn Slobodian, “The sovereign individual in Downing Street”, New Statesman, 2 November 2022; Harrison Smith and Roger Burrows, “Software, Sovereignty and the Post-Neoliberal Politics of Exit”, Theory, Culture & Society 38 (6), 2021

1994Claimed ancestor · cited

“Meltdown”

Nick Land

In spring 1994 a philosophy lecturer at Warwick gives a talk called “Meltdown” at a Virtual Futures conference. It begins with a story, in which “Earth is captured by a technocapital singularity”. The text draws on Deleuze and Guattari's Anti-Oedipus, which it cites. e/acc's 2022 tenets link to it when they note that Land saw capitalism as a form of intelligence.

Nick Land, “Meltdown”, Virtual Futures conference, University of Warwick, 1994, text on the CCRU website; James Duesterberg, “Silicon Valley's Favorite Doomsaying Philosopher”, The New Yorker, 19 February 2026; Beff Jezos and Bayeslord, “Notes on e/acc principles and tenets”, 2022

1970s

1972Claimed ancestor · cited

Anti-Oedipus

Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari

A philosopher and a psychoanalyst publish the first volume of Capitalism and Schizophrenia. In one passage they ask whether the revolutionary path is to withdraw from the world market or to go further into it. They suggest the second, to “accelerate the process”, as Nietzsche put it. Nick Land's 2017 introduction to accelerationism quotes the passage in full.

Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, L'Anti-Œdipe. Capitalisme et schizophrénie, Éditions de Minuit, 1972; English translation by Robert Hurley, Mark Seem and Helen R. Lane, Viking, 1977; Nick Land, “A Quick-and-Dirty Introduction to Accelerationism”, Jacobite, 25 May 2017

1960s

1964Claimed ancestor · cited

Types of civilisation

Nikolai Kardashev

Writing on how alien civilisations might send signals, a Soviet astrophysicist ranks them by the power they command. Type I commands roughly the power of a planet, Type II that of its star, Type III that of its galaxy. The e/acc newsletter's first goal is to harness more energy and climb “the Kardashev gradient”. Another is to build AI.

N. S. Kardashev, “Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations”, Astronomicheskii Zhurnal, 1964, translated in Soviet Astronomy 8, 1964, p. 217; e/acc, “What the F* is e/acc”, e/acc newsletter, Substack, 26 December 2022

1900s

1909Attributed by historians

The Founding and Manifesto of Futurism

Filippo Tommaso Marinetti

On the front page of Le Figaro, the Italian poet launches Futurism. His manifesto rejects the past and glorifies speed, machines, youth and violence, treating war as a kind of hygiene. Marinetti later became active in Fascist politics. The critic Benjamin Noys, who coined the term accelerationism, compares it with Futurism, whose fate he reads as a warning.

F. T. Marinetti, “Le Futurisme”, Le Figaro, Paris, 20 February 1909 (front page, MIT Libraries Dome); Benjamin Noys, “The Corpse of Accelerationism”, e-flux Notes, 14 November 2025
