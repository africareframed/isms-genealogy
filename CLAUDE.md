# A genealogy of the isms behind artificial intelligence

Instructions for every Claude session and agent working in this repository. Read `WORKPLAN.md` next: it says what is done and what comes next.

## The project

A website, built first to pitch funders for an exhibition. It maps the isms of the AI industry and traces each one back in time. Owner: Thomas (GitHub account `africareframed`). The site is called **Radical Romance** (owner, 25 November 2026: "Made Minds", its name from 20 November, is dropped altogether; never use it again), always with the subtitle "A genealogy of the ideas behind AI" (changed on 20 November 2026 at the owner's request; it was "A genealogy of the isms behind artificial intelligence"). The domain is the owner's to check and buy (mademinds.org is no longer the plan).

**Never open, read, clone or change `africareframed/africareframed-site` or any other repository.** Only this one.

## The framework (decided, do not change without the owner)

- **An ism** is mapped when it is a whole view of AI (what it is, where it is heading, what to do), born in or against the tech world (widened by the owner on 22 Nov 2026 for the Protection family), and named by its own people, with founding texts, vocabulary and institutions to trace.
- **Twelve movements in six families** (owner, 25 Nov 2026: the new framework, worked out in his doc "A new framing of the AI movements", https://claude.ai/artifact/JZZanMGfFdDsTYLQWH2bHi; its main tab is saved as `docs/framework/framing.md`). A family is named by a verb for what it wants done; families run from the largest claims about what AI will become to the smallest; each movement sits next to the one it shares most people with. Numbered 01–12 in this order:
  - Transcend: Transhumanism (includes longevity and Don't Die, cryonics, extropianism) · Singularitarianism (includes successionism)
  - Accelerate: Techno-optimism (includes effective accelerationism, the network state, progress studies, the abundance agenda, augmentation) · American Dynamism (includes a national AGI project, AI dominance, the technological republic)
  - Prevent: AI safety (includes alignment research, lab safety and responsible scaling, AI governance, effective altruism and longtermism, Humanist AI) · Pause (includes rationalism, the stop movement, the superintelligence ban)
  - Protect: Pro-human (includes humane technology, the families' campaigns, faith-based humanism) · AI ethics (includes algorithmic justice, the ghost-work critique). Protect shields people living now; Prevent guards humanity against a machine that may come.
  - Reclaim: Digital sovereignty (includes the anti-monopoly movement, public AI) · Neo-Luddism (includes the analogue revival, Marxist neo-Luddism). Reclaim changes who owns and decides: build our own, or refuse.
  - Doubt: Normal technology (includes the snake-oil critique of predictive AI, the modest-growth economics) · AI skepticism (includes the hype critique, the neurosymbolic critique, the bubble thesis). Doubt is not a critics category: it holds only those whose whole view is that AI is ordinary or oversold.
  - Rules: a **movement** is a whole view of AI, called by its own people's name, with founding texts, institutions and vocabulary; two movements in one family differ in what they value or fear. A **strand** ("Includes") shares its movement's demand and fears but may ground its values differently. Retired as movements on 25 Nov 2026 and now strands: effective accelerationism (Techno-optimism), rationalism (Pause), effective altruism (AI safety); their old addresses redirect. American Dynamism and Digital sovereignty are kept apart on purpose: the view from the top of the stack and from below.
- **Below the twelve: key words and the vocabulary** (owner, 20 Nov 2026). The broad themes are gone. Instead, narrow terms people hear again and again in the debate. Every term is in the **vocabulary** (`src/data/words/`: what it means, who coined it and when, which isms use it). About a dozen are **key words** with an illustrated card and their own page (`src/content/themes/`, the old theme files): Scaling laws (Perceptron card), Compute (H100 chips), Abundance (meter), Augmentation (mouse), Facial recognition (camera), Citizens' assemblies (kleroterion), Luddite (the Luddite engraving); to come with new cards: Open weights, Alignment, AGI, The pause, p(doom). No separate critics section: criticism sits in each page's "Who disputes it" and in narrow words of its own. More key words are added over time; each stays narrow.
- **Four tracks** (owner, 20 Nov 2026; People added 23 Nov 2026), in this order: **History of AI** (what happened: machines, ideas, labs, money, laws; the ground all eight react to), **Movements** (who believes what), **People** (who the actors are and how they connect) and **Vocabulary** (the words they fight with). The menu shows these four in this order. The history links the other two by data: each moment is tagged with movements and key words; each era shows the movements named in it (`born` on each ism) and the words coined in it (by year); movement and key-word pages list "In the history of AI".

A hard cut, no flicker. The pieces: Deep Blue over Kasparov's face, a grey slip beside the square, the man inside the Turk, an orange slip, a square of Lee Sedol. The whole image is the way into the history: a click anywhere on it, pieces included, opens the history page (owner: a second meaning for a click confused it). - Design follows the mockup (canvas "AI philosophies — website mockup"): white, Inter Tight + IBM Plex Mono, one accent `#D8461C`. Super clean, large type with presence, short texts.
- **One grid** (owner, 21 Nov 2026): 12 columns with one gutter inside the 1312 px container (`--g` 40 px; 32 px under 1100 px, 20 px under 640 px). Every layout is built from spans, so column edges fall on the same vertical lines on every page: movement rows 6 + 6, hero 5 + an empty column + 6, history card 5 + 7, page introductions 7 + 5, key-word cards and the era index 3 + 3 + 3 + 3, figures 4 + 4 + 4, the movements' rows on a key-word page 4 + 8. A span of n columns is `calc(var(--c) * n + var(--g) * (n - 1))`; equal splits use `repeat(n, 1fr)` with `column-gap: var(--g)`.

## Writing rules

- British English. Plain words, sentences under 25 words, paragraphs of at most three sentences.
- Introduction: a lead sentence of what the ism holds today, then two short paragraphs that may reach into the past. Fulfilling, never long.
- **History of AI moments** (owner, 23 Nov 2026; only the History of AI, not the movement timelines): up to six sentences; a moment that tells one story of several events up to eight. Paragraphs of at most three sentences, separated by a blank line in the YAML text. Written for a curious outsider: explain jargon and people in passing, give the concrete detail that makes it real (a number, a name, what it looked like), and say why it mattered and what it set off. Keep the site's own calm, plain tone (not a podcast's voice), neutral, critics given their say.
- **One story, one moment** (owner, 23 Nov 2026): when a later event continues an earlier one (the same question or actors, within about a year), it goes into the same moment, dated by the first event and told in order: what happened, how people reacted, what followed. Titles name the event ("The swarm attack", not "The swarm").
- **Statements** (owner, 23 Nov 2026): a text or declaration goes into the History of AI only when it became an event (governments, labs or the public acted on it, or it gave the debate words it still uses); other manifestos belong on their movement's timeline.
- Timeline entries: a title, who, and two to four sentences. Give `month` (1–12) when two entries share a year, so they sort newest first. Ten to sixteen entries per ism, from today back to its oldest documented root.
- Every entry has a real, specific source (author, title, publisher or venue, year) and, where possible, `sourceUrl` to a page you actually opened. Never invent a source.
- Mark each root's relation: `claimed` (the movement cites it as an ancestor), `attributed` (historians link it, the movement does not), `coined`, `definition`, `institutional-root`, `milestone`, `reacted-against`.
- Neutral and fair. Describe what people said and did; give critics their own section. No verdicts.
- Quotations: at most one per source, under 15 words. Paraphrase everything else.
- **Lenses** (new setup, owner 25 Nov 2026): Values and Fears as short stated terms. Promises: the movement's ideal and the tradition it echoes, then the first consequences, one step from its demands, written in lower case and the conditional to follow "If <movement>'s demands became law tomorrow,". Interests, written by hand as prose, always in this order: the funding (public record, or that none was found); then, after a bold "Its demands could serve", who stands to gain and why; who would lose; the opponents who could gain anyway. Funders are named only in the funding part, beneficiaries only after it. Interest, never motive: "could serve", never "is behind".
- New text is `status: draft`. A separate checker agent, which has not seen the drafting, checks every claim against the sources and sets `status: checked` only when it holds.

## Image rules

- **Never AI-generated.** Human-made images only: documents, title pages, diagrams, machines, places, artworks, photographs of objects or events.
- **No portraits or photographs of real people**, whatever their licence (owner, 20 Nov 2026: too many people, too many images). Notable figures appear as names only: an index on each ism page where each name folds open to a short note and a link to its moment in the history. One exception (owner, 21 Nov 2026): the History of AI card and its timeline moments show Garry Kasparov (1997) and Lee Sedol (2016) from broadcast stills, which the owner cleared as fair use. Also at his say (22–23 Nov 2026): the Protection cards, from recent photographs: protesters outside OpenAI (Unsplash), Bernie Sanders at the Pro-Human Assembly (a still the owner supplied) and the AFP head-cam photos of Indian workers recording training data (cleared by the owner as an artistic treatment). No other images of people without the owner's say. **Portraits** (owner, 23 Nov 2026, 04:50): every file in his folder `isms-genealogy-images/portraits/` (213 portraits, listed in its `portraits.csv`) is cleared, whatever the CSV's licence column says (fair use, external web source); record them `rights: cleared`, with the CSV's licence or source as the visible credit. The owner started the People track on 23 Nov 2026: the portraits show there, in black and white through CSS, each with its credit. Twelve showed the wrong person or none and are left out, with Walter Pitts and Amanda Askell (group photos) and Jeremy England (source unclear), until replaced (list in the folder's `portrait-check/portrait-check.csv`). Elsewhere on the site the rule above still holds.
- Record every image in `src/data/images/` with title, date, rights (`public-domain`, `open-licence` + licence, `cleared`, `to-check`) and `sourceUrl` of the page where the licence was read. `licence` is the credit readers see in the caption, so it names the source, never who cleared it; clearance notes go in `note`, which is never shown (owner, 21 Nov 2026).
- Prefer Wikimedia Commons, the Library of Congress, the Internet Archive and museum open-access collections. Copyrighted items (book covers, press photos, film stills) stay `to-check` placeholders until the owner clears them.
- Downloads happen only after the owner approves a batch (a list of files with source, size and licence). They go to the owner's connected folder `isms-genealogy-images` and are then copied into `public/images/`.

### Collages (the house image style, from 20 Nov 2026)

**Why collage** (owner, 21 Nov 2026): AI is itself a kind of collage. Its training material is a collage of human knowledge, and in the AI's reality we are becoming a collage too. AI hides its seams; our collages show them: human-made fragments, cut along the grid, out of register, each with its year and source. This is the reason behind every rule below.

The full recipe lives in the design system **Radical Romance Illustration** (renamed from Made Minds Illustration on 25 Nov 2026) (https://claude.ai/artifact/AKayCErJw2u8QYjb3bzDSN, slug `4b7a91e1-db02-4604-a0e6-7241adc16c37`): rules, the step-by-step recipe, the seven theme cards as references, the owner's decisions and the versions he turned down. Read its `project/README.md` and `project/01-recipe.md` before making or changing a collage. When the owner changes the style, update the guide first, then the engine. It holds all sixteen cards (eight movements in highlighter family colours, eight key words in grey), the History of AI card and its motion, and the family colours as tokens (updated 23 Nov 2026, version 1795389060-6ef0; family tokens renamed to the six families and Doubt added on 25 Nov 2026).

Every ism and theme card is a collage made in code by `scripts/collages.py` (`python3 scripts/collages.py <id>`), from archive images already cleared. Rules: archive fragments only (objects, drawings, engravings, machines, charts; no title pages or text documents); fragments cut along a 100 px grid, with a few squares shifted, inverted or removed; two bold colour fields under them, slightly out of register; family colour for isms, always a highlighter colour (owner, 22 Nov 2026: Transcend `#C9F25B`, Accelerate `#FF9F4F`, Prevent `#F5A3BC`, Protect `#73C7F2`, Reclaim `#C3A6FF` (24 Nov 2026), Doubt `#2EE6C8` (turquoise, 25 Nov 2026; yellow was turned down as too close to Transcend's lime); `#D8461C` stays the site's one accent and the History of AI card's orange), grey for themes; type only when it carries meaning (a date, a word of the ism's own) and then always whole, never cut; small mono labels with year and source on paper strips. Guiding rule (owner, 20 Nov): one strong object, one exact fact, plenty of air, as in the Scale collage (the Perceptron with 10²⁵). Big type is rare (owner, 20 Nov): most cards have none, and type never captions its object (no "$500" by a chip, no "0 kWh" by a meter). It earns its place only when it comes from another time than the object, so the viewer closes the gap. A concrete object or a figure in action beats a symbol or an artwork. Each collage's record in `src/data/images/collages.yaml` names its sources and the licence that follows from them. Every card comes in two formats: the card for the grid (`collage-<id>`, 1500 × 1000 for themes) and a vertical page image (`collage-<id>-tall`, 1200 × 1500, 4:5) composed again for the top of its page and set as `pageImage`. Never stretch a photograph: the box keeps the crop's proportions (the engine warns).

## Build and check

```
npm install
npm run build      # must pass with no warnings before any commit
```

The build is flat `.html` files with relative links, so `dist/` works as a private preview artifact, on GitHub Pages or on a domain.

The owner made the site public on 20 November 2026: the repository is public and GitHub Pages serves the `gh-pages` branch at https://africareframed.github.io/isms-genealogy/. After merging into `main`, run `npm run deploy` to update it. Every page carries `noindex` until the owner launches it properly. Anything new that is public in a different way (a domain, search indexing, announcements) still needs the owner's explicit go.

## Git

Commit small, with clear messages. Agents working on one ism use a branch `ism/<ism>` and push it; the coordinating session merges into `main` after the build passes.
