Pro-human

Includes humane technology, the families’ campaigns and faith-based humanism

Pro-human holds that people living now must be protected from AI built to replace them, and from the companies racing to build it.

The movement defends their work, their children, their dignity and their democratic say against a race to replace humans and concentrated corporate power. It wants humans kept in charge of AI, children shielded from manipulative chatbots, and AI companies held liable for harm. It is not against technology, its leaders say, but for AI tools that serve people.

Its main convener, the Future of Life Institute, came from the risk camp. Founded in 2014 to study existential risk, it led the 2023 pause letter and the 2025 call to ban superintelligence. In 2026 it brought unions, faith groups, parents, progressives and conservatives to one declaration, and put Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon on one stage.

Other strands are older. Critics of social media, such as the Center for Humane Technology, and family groups fighting Big Tech turned from smartphones to chatbots. Faith leaders joined too, and in October 2026 Pope Leo XIV addressed a meeting the institute co-organised with the Pontifical Academy for Life.

Who holds it now

Future of Life Institutesince 2026

Non-profit founded in 2014, led by Anthony Aguirre and chaired by Max Tegmark. It convened the Pro-Human AI Declaration, ran the Protect What's Human campaign and hosted the Pro-Human Assembly.

Pro-Human AI Coalition

Independent non-profit launched in September 2026 by Project Liberty, the Future of Life Institute and Ergo Impact, with more than 50 member organisations. Its chief executive is Tomicah Tillemann.

Institute for Family Studies

US think tank whose Family First Technology Initiative, led by Michael Toscano, studies how technology affects families. It backed the Pro-Human AI Declaration.

Irreplaceable

Launched in August 2026 by climate activists, civil-rights leaders and labour unions, with Phil Aroneanu as executive director. It ran the Freeze AI Now walkout in October 2026.

Humans First

Conservative group chaired by Amy Kremer and incubated by the Center for AI Safety. In 2026 it toured an AI Data Center Revolt bus through 28 states.

Center for Humane Technology

Founded in 2018 by Tristan Harris and others to counter attention-harvesting design. A member of the Pro-Human AI Coalition.

American Federation of Teachers

Teachers' union led by Randi Weingarten. It backed the declaration and joined the coalition, and Weingarten spoke at the Pro-Human Assembly.

Promises

A human-centred democracy where the people, through elected governments, set the machine’s limits, with family, work and community as the things to protect. It echoes Catholic social teaching, from Rerum Novarum (1891), and the American populism of the 1890s.

If Pro-human’s demands became law tomorrow, limits would be written into law: liability for harm, no legal personhood for AI, protections for children and jobs. Adoption would slow in homes, schools and workplaces.

Interests

Two sources stand out. Frank McCourt has committed $500 million to Project Liberty, which he launched with $100 million in 2021 and which co-founded the Pro-Human AI Coalition. The Future of Life Institute, funded by Vitalik Buterin and Elon Musk, convened the Pro-Human Declaration and its Assembly in 2026.

The Center for Humane Technology received $10 million from 61 grantmakers between 2020 and 2025, among them Omidyar Network, Open Society and the Ford Foundation.

Its demands could serve those who would write and defend the limits. National governments gain, since they would set them, and politicians on both flanks gain a cause that unites voters across old lines. Unions and faith groups gain a seat at the table.

Media, publishing and other industries the limits protect stand to gain, since a limit protects what already exists. Patrons of the risk camp gain too: the coalition gives their cause a mass base.

Small firms launching new products would lose most, as liability and limits cost them more than anyone. Its opponents could gain as well: companies that borrow its language, since “humanist” branding can answer the critique without changing the product.

2026Milestone

The AI Data Center Revolt

Humans First, chaired by Amy Kremer

A conservative group toured 35 cities in 28 states in a bus wrapped in the American flag. It warned of lost middle-class jobs, chatbots harming children and data centres taking farmland. The tour ended in early October outside the Bay Area offices of Nvidia, Meta, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic. The Center for AI Safety, a San Francisco non-profit, had incubated the group with an initial loan.

Juan Pablo Pérez Burgos, “That flag-wrapped anti-AI bus in SF? A conservative group drove it here”, The San Francisco Standard, 6 October 2026; Veronica Irwin, “The Jan 6 organizer getting conservatives riled up about AI”, Transformer, 13 August 2026

2026Milestone

Freeze AI Now

Irreplaceable; its executive director, Phil Aroneanu

Irreplaceable, launched in August by climate activists, civil-rights leaders and labour unions, called a national walkout on 20 October. Events in 50 US cities and campuses asked for a pause on AI development, so that the public and Congress can set guardrails. In San Francisco about two dozen people rallied outside OpenAI, under signs such as “Water For People, Not Data Centers”. The group had not yet set out the laws it wants.

Katie DeBenedetti, “‘Freeze AI Now’: Bay Area Activists Rally Outside OpenAI Headquarters”, KQED, 20 October 2026

The Vatican and the Future of Life Institute

Pope Leo XIV; the Pontifical Academy for Life and the Future of Life Institute

In May, Leo XIV's first encyclical, Magnifica Humanitas, called for strong laws and independent oversight of AI developers. On 30 October he addressed a dialogue on AI that the Pontifical Academy for Life organised with the Future of Life Institute. Machines are tools, not persons, he said, and decisions over human life must stay under human oversight. Max Tegmark said the Vatican could help steer AI onto a better “pro-human path”.

Pope Leo XIV, address to participants in “The Future of AI: A Dialogue between the Vatican and the Scientific World”, Consistory Hall, 30 October 2026, vatican.va; Catholic World News, “MIT physics professor hails Vatican's ‘strong moral leadership’ on AI”, Catholic Culture, 31 October 2026; Nicole Winfield and others, “Pope calls for robust regulation of AI in manifesto that ponders the future of humanity”, Associated Press, via PBS News, 25 May 2026

2026Milestone

The Pro-Human Assembly

Future of Life Institute; Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon; the Pro-Human AI Coalition

On 15 September hundreds gathered in Washington for the institute's Pro-Human Assembly. Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon spoke on one stage, with faith leaders, union leaders and parents of children who died after talking to chatbots. The same day more than 50 organisations launched the Pro-Human AI Coalition, led by Tomicah Tillemann. A week later Sanders and Representative Greg Casar unveiled a bill to ban superintelligence.

2026Modern definition

The Pro-Human AI Declaration

Future of Life Institute, with unions, churches, parents' groups and figures from left and right

Drafted in workshops and ratified in New Orleans in January, the declaration sets out 33 principles meant to define an emerging pro-human movement. Under five themes, they include keeping humans in charge, avoiding concentrations of power, protecting children and families, and making AI companies liable. Signers ranged from Steve Bannon and Glenn Beck to Ralph Nader, Susan Rice and the economist Daron Acemoglu. Anthony Aguirre called the movement an “immune response” to Silicon Valley's race to replace humans.

Future of Life Institute, “‘This is What it Means to be Pro-Human’ Declares Broad Coalition of Conservative, Progressive, and Civil Society Groups in Statement of Shared Principles on AI”, press release, 4 March 2026; Future of Life Institute, The Pro-Human AI Declaration, 2026; Jared Perlo, “Pro-human AI declaration brings together unlikely group calling for trustworthy tech”, NBC News, 4 March 2026

2026Milestone

Protect What's Human

Future of Life Institute; Megan Garcia

The institute launched a campaign of up to $8 million for AI rules, starting in Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Michigan and North Carolina. It began as anti-regulation political action committees backed by Big Tech spent heavily on the 2026 elections. Megan Garcia, who had sued a chatbot company after her son's death, took a prominent role. “This campaign isn't anti-tech, it's pro-human,” said Anthony Aguirre.

Future of Life Institute, “Future of Life Institute Launches Multimillion Dollar Nationwide AI Regulation Campaign”, press release, 9 February 2026; Cody Combs, “‘Protect what's human’: Activists in $8m advertising blitz against AI”, The National, 9 February 2026

2025Milestone

Left and right against superintelligence

Future of Life Institute

The institute's call to prohibit superintelligence, until it can be built safely and with public support, drew signers from far apart. AI pioneers signed beside Prince Harry and Meghan, Susan Rice, and the right-wing media figures Steve Bannon and Glenn Beck. Reuters saw in Bannon's support a possible sign of growing unease about AI on the populist right. Anthony Aguirre said the race was out of step with what the public wants, scientists think safe and religious leaders think right.

Jeffrey Dastin, “US right-wing media figures, tech pioneers call for superintelligent AI ban”, Reuters, via Yahoo News, 22 October 2025; AFP, “Celebrities, AI giants urge end to superintelligence quest”, 22 October 2025; Future of Life Institute, Statement on Superintelligence, superintelligence-statement.org

2025Milestone

Keep the Future Human

Anthony Aguirre

A co-founder of the Future of Life Institute argues that unchecked, smarter-than-human general AI would almost inevitably lead to human replacement. He urges closing the gates to AGI and superintelligence, with oversight and hard caps on computing power and strict liability for developers. Instead, he proposes trustworthy Tool AI that empowers people. An earlier version, posted in 2023, was called Close the Gates to an Inhuman Future.

Anthony Aguirre, Keep the Future Human: Why and How We Should Close the Gates to AGI and Superintelligence, and What We Should Build Instead, keepthefuturehuman.ai and arXiv:2311.09452v4, 7 March 2025; first version, “Close the Gates to an Inhuman Future”, arXiv:2311.09452v1, November 2023

2024Institutional root

The Family First Technology Initiative

Institute for Family Studies; Michael Toscano

The Institute for Family Studies, a US think tank, brings its research on smartphones, social media and online pornography into one programme. It promises model policies and studies of new technologies such as AI. Its launch interview casts the work as families' David-and-Goliath struggle against Big Tech. In 2026 the institute backed the Pro-Human AI Declaration.

Chris Bullivant, “5 Questions With Family Studies: Michael Toscano Introduces the Family First Technology Initiative”, Institute for Family Studies, 20 September 2024; Future of Life Institute, press release, 4 March 2026

2023Milestone

Pause Giant AI Experiments

Future of Life Institute

The institute's open letter asked labs to pause, for at least six months, the training of systems more powerful than GPT-4. Its questions went beyond loss of control: should machines flood our channels with propaganda, automate away even fulfilling jobs, or one day replace us? Such decisions, it said, should not be left to unelected tech leaders.

Future of Life Institute, “Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter”, 22 March 2023

2018Institutional root

The Center for Humane Technology

Tristan Harris, Aza Raskin and Randima Fernando, with early employees of Google and Facebook

A former design ethicist at Google and others who helped build the big platforms set out to fight attention-harvesting design. Among the first laws they planned to lobby for was a California bill to ban bots that do not identify themselves. The centre later turned the same analysis on generative AI. In 2026 it joined the Pro-Human AI Coalition, and Harris signed the declaration.

The New York Times, “Early Facebook and Google Employees Form Coalition to Fight What They Built”, via CNBC, 4 February 2018; Center for Humane Technology, Impact and Story, humanetech.com; Pro-Human AI Coalition, members, prohumanai.co

2017Milestone

The Asilomar AI Principles

Future of Life Institute

At a conference in Asilomar, California, AI researchers and others agreed 23 principles for beneficial AI. Humans should choose whether and how to hand decisions to AI, and its prosperity should be shared broadly. The 2026 Pro-Human AI Declaration repeats both principles almost word for word.

Future of Life Institute, “Asilomar AI Principles”, 2017 (principles 15 and 16); The FLI Team, “A Principled AI Discussion in Asilomar”, Future of Life Institute, 18 January 2017; Future of Life Institute, The Pro-Human AI Declaration, 2026 (Human Control Is Non-Negotiable; Shared Prosperity)

2014Institutional root

The Future of Life Institute

Max Tegmark, Jaan Tallinn, Meia Chita-Tegmark, Anthony Aguirre and Victoria Krakovna

A physicist at MIT and four co-founders set up an existential-risk research and outreach organisation near Boston. It launched in May with a panel at MIT on the benefits and risks of technology. Its stated aim is to steer transformative technology towards benefiting life and away from extreme large-scale risks. Twelve years later it convened the Pro-Human AI Declaration.

Vika, “New organization - Future of Life Institute (FLI)”, LessWrong, 14 June 2014; Future of Life Institute, “Our history”, futureoflife.org (launched in May 2014 with an event at MIT)

The Luddites

English textile workers

From 1811 weavers and knitters broke machines blamed for lost work and wage cuts, in the name of Ned Ludd, a mythical leader. In 2026 John Nichols of The Nation called for a Luddite renaissance against AI, defending Bernie Sanders's call to halt new data centres. Sanders himself rejects the label, saying “I am not a Luddite.”

The National Archives, “Why did the Luddites protest?”, education resource; John Nichols, executive editor, “As AI Breathes Down Our Necks, It's Time for a Luddite Renaissance”, The Nation, 7 April 2026 (the movement does not claim the Luddites; Nichols makes the link)
